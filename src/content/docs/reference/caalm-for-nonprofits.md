This page is a **brochure-style introduction** for nonprofit teams across the country. It explains what CAALM does for mission-driven organizations—not just what buttons to click.

CAALM stands for **Compliance and Agreement Lifecycle Management**. In everyday terms, it is the place your team keeps **contracts, licenses, audits, and the workflows** that stop agreements from quietly expiring. That work sits alongside CAALM’s **nonprofit fundraising** tools (donors, gifts, volunteers, and your public give page).

> [!NOTE]
> CAALM does not replace lawyers, auditors, or grant writers. It helps your staff **see, own, and act on** the agreements and credentials that funders and governments expect you to manage well.

## Who this is written for

- **Small and mid-size nonprofits** with thin admin staff (one person often handles grants, HR, and facilities)
- **Human services, health, education, and community development** organizations that hold **government grants, vendor contracts, and professional licenses**
- **Board members and executives** who want honest answers about how AI fits CAALM—not hype

If you already use CAALM, treat this as a story you can share with new leaders. For step-by-step product guides, see [Funding and retention](/docs/reference/funding-retention), [Audit readiness](/docs/reference/audit-readiness), and [CAALM Assistant](/docs/reference/assistant).

## The problem CAALM addresses

Without a system of record, nonprofits often:

- Store contract PDFs in email, personal drives, or shared folders
- Track renewals in spreadsheets and calendar reminders
- Discover expired licenses or missed reports **after** a funder or regulator notices
- Struggle to answer “Who owns this agreement?” in an RFP or audit

That pattern is common nationwide. It is also how organizations **lose funding they already had**—and look **riskier** than peers when bidding for the next contract.

CAALM centralizes **contracts, licenses, files, approvals, calendar deadlines, and audit evidence** so your team can act **before** deadlines become emergencies.

## How built-in AI helps your nonprofit day to day

CAALM’s AI features are **permission-gated** (only people your admin allows can use them) and work best when your agreements and licenses **live in CAALM**, not only on someone’s laptop.

### Less typing when PDFs arrive

Grants, subcontracts, MOUs, lease addenda, and license renewal letters still show up as PDFs.

**What AI does:** On intake, **AI document extraction** pulls key fields—parties, dates, amounts, renewal language—so staff review and fix instead of retyping everything.

**Why it matters:** A part-time operations lead should not spend weekends on data entry. Plans include a monthly extraction allowance (see [public pricing](/PRICING.md)—for example **50 extractions per month on Starter** and **500 on Growth**).

### Contract assistant—plain-language Q&A on one agreement

Built for **nonprofit, government, and vendor** agreements—not generic corporate boilerplate.

**What AI does:** After a contract is in CAALM, your team can ask questions like “When can we terminate?” or “What reporting is due?” and get answers tied to **page references** in the PDF.

**Why it matters:** Program directors and coordinators can sanity-check a deal **before** calling outside counsel on every renewal. The assistant **does not give legal advice**; it helps you know **what to ask** and **what to calendar**.

### CAALM Assistant—help that uses your live data

The in-app **CAALM Assistant** can, when your role allows:

- Search **your organization’s** contracts and licenses  
- List **pending tasks** and approvals  
- Show **calendar** items and help **schedule** reviews or deadline meetings  
- Explain **how to use CAALM** on the page you are on  

**Why it matters:** A twelve-person nonprofit cannot memorize five systems. A director can ask “What licenses expire this month?” in normal language instead of clicking through every menu.

### Audit readiness AI—prep for reviews, not a pass/fail grade

On **[Audit readiness](/docs/reference/audit-readiness)**, AI summarizes **gaps from contracts and licenses already in CAALM** and suggests practical follow-up questions (for example, evidence prep for health-center or human-services monitoring contexts).

**Why it matters:** Funders and licensors often ask for proof that you run a tight shop. Readiness turns “we think we’re ready” into a **short list of fixes**—missing owner, at-risk license, contract inside a renewal window.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CAALM readiness is not an official audit.** Scores and packets reflect **your data in CAALM**. They are not a federal, state, funder, or CPA determination.

## Winning contracts—and standing apart from peer organizations

CAALM is **not** a magic “win the RFP” tool. When two nonprofits bid on the same **state, county, or federal** service line, buyers weigh **price, past performance, staffing and licenses, and operational risk**. CAALM helps on **capture discipline, speed, and proof**—the back office that decision-makers notice when scores are close.

### Pursuits: treat bids like a pipeline

**[Funding and retention](/docs/reference/funding-retention)** includes a **Pursuits** board: watch a lead, qualify it, pursue it, submit, then mark **won** or **lost**. Leads can start manually or from **[SAM.gov search](/docs/reference/sam-gov)** inside CAALM.

When you mark a pursuit **won**, you can **spawn a proposal** into **[Approvals](/docs/reference/approvals)** instead of restarting in email and Word.

**Why it sets you apart:** Many small nonprofits submit late or hand off chaotically. A visible pipeline and one path from **win → proposal** looks more mature to your board and partners—and helps you miss fewer deadlines.

### AI on agreements: fewer missed requirements

**Extraction** and the **contract assistant** help staff read subcontract and RFP terms faster: reporting dates, insurance, audit clauses, termination.

**Why it sets you apart:** Peers often lose points for **“we didn’t see that requirement.”** AI does not write your narrative; it reduces costly oversights.

### Templates, clause library, and negotiate: look organized

Reuse approved wording in **[Contract templates](/docs/reference/contract-templates)** and the **[Clause library](/docs/reference/clause-library)**. Keep redlines in **[Negotiate](/docs/reference/negotiate)**, then send for formal review.

**Why it sets you apart:** Buyers compare vendors that feel **organized** versus **ad hoc**. Cleaner submittals and an audit trail of who approved what support **past performance** stories.

### Retention: protect the contracts that become your track record

The **retention** side of Funding & Retention ranks **dollars at risk**, ties **obligations** (reports, renewals, deliverables) to live contracts, and keeps owners and dates current.

**Why it sets you apart:** The next RFP asks how you performed on the **last** award. Organizations that miss renewals or reports have weak references. CAALM helps you **keep** the wins you already earned.

### Readiness and evidence: show you can operate, not only promise

**Audit readiness** scores live contracts and licenses, stores snapshots over time, and can export a **shareable packet**. Pair that with **[Files](/docs/reference/files)** and **[Licenses](/docs/reference/licenses)** so credentials and supporting documents are findable—not “in someone’s inbox.”

**Why it sets you apart:** In licensed and grant-funded work, procurement favors vendors who can show **current licenses, owned contracts, and evidence**—not promises alone.

### Fundraising tools on the same platform

Nonprofit teams also run **[Constituents](/docs/reference/constituents)**, **[Gifts and campaigns](/docs/reference/gifts-and-campaigns)**, the **[Public donation page](/docs/reference/donation-page)**, and **[Volunteers](/docs/reference/volunteers)**. **[Nonprofit finance settings](/docs/reference/nonprofit-finance-settings)** support restricted-fund and Form 990 **worksheet** handoff to your preparer (not payroll, general ledger, or e-file).

That combination matters when you tell one story to **grant officers** (contracts and compliance) and another to **donors** (giving and stewardship)—without duplicating people records in conflicting spreadsheets.

## What AI does not do (honest expectations)

- It does **not** write your mission statement, community partnerships, or competitive price.  
- It does **not** guarantee grant or contract awards.  
- It requires **admin configuration** and **permissions**; if AI is disabled in your environment, core CAALM still works without these features.  
- **Extraction limits** apply by plan; overages block until you upgrade or the next billing cycle resets capacity.  
- **Winning** still depends on program quality, relationships, and outcomes. CAALM makes it easier to **prove** performance and **execute** after you win.
